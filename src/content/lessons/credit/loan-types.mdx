<WhyItMatters>
Not all debt is equally dangerous. A federal student loan with income-based repayment options is very different from a payday loan. Knowing the differences helps you borrow only when it makes sense and pick the least expensive option when you do. With a master's starting in August 2027, student loans are the type to understand first.
</WhyItMatters>

## Student loans

**Federal student loans** come from the U.S. Department of Education. Their rates are fixed and set by law each year, and they come with protections private loans usually don't have, like income-driven repayment and forgiveness programs <Cite id="studentaid-rates" />. **Private student loans** come from banks and other lenders. Their rates depend on your credit (or a co-signer's), and their protections vary.

For graduate school, federal Direct Unsubsidized Loans are the main option. "Unsubsidized" means interest starts building while you're in school. Subsidized loans, where the government covers interest while you're enrolled, are only for undergraduates <Cite id="studentaid-loans" />.

<Uncertain>
Federal graduate borrowing rules changed for loans made after July 1, 2026. Grad PLUS loans are no longer available to new borrowers, and new annual and lifetime limits apply to graduate loans. Check the current limits on StudentAid.gov before you plan how to pay for your master's <Cite id="studentaid-loans" />.
</Uncertain>

### Public Service Loan Forgiveness

<Term id="pslf">PSLF</Term> forgives the remaining balance on federal Direct Loans after 120 qualifying monthly payments (10 years' worth) while you work full time for a government agency or a qualifying nonprofit <Cite id="studentaid-pslf" />. City and county planning departments are government employers, and many universities are public or nonprofit. If you borrow for your master's and then work in public-sector planning, PSLF could be worth a lot. The payments don't need to be consecutive, but they must be on a qualifying repayment plan, and you should certify your employment regularly <Cite id="studentaid-pslf" />.

## Credit cards

Credit cards are convenient and, paid in full each month, free. Carried balances are some of the most expensive common debt, typically above 20% <Cite id="fed-g19" />. See [How interest and loans work](/credit/how-interest-works/).

## Auto loans

Car loans are secured by the car, which means it can be repossessed if you stop paying. Before shopping, the CFPB suggests getting preapproved by a bank or credit union so you can compare it with the dealer's offer, and focusing on the total cost rather than the monthly payment <Cite id="cfpb-auto-loans" />.

<Example title="Worked example: stretching a car loan">
You borrow $30,000 at 7% APR.

| Loan length | Monthly payment | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 years | $926 | $3,347 |
| 4 years | $718 | $4,483 |
| 6 years | $511 | $6,826 |

The six-year loan "feels" cheaper but costs about **twice as much interest** as the three-year one. And because cars lose value quickly, a long loan can leave you owing more than the car is worth <Cite id="cfpb-auto-loans" />.
</Example>

## Mortgages

A mortgage is a loan to buy a home, usually over 15 or 30 years, secured by the home itself. See [Mortgages and rent vs. buy](/credit/rent-vs-buy/).

## Buy now, pay later (BNPL)

BNPL services split a purchase into a few payments, often four payments over six weeks with no interest. The CFPB has found that they're easy to stack across several apps, can charge late fees, and encourage spending more than planned <Cite id="cfpb-bnpl-2022" />. Treat them like any other debt: only if you'd buy the item with cash today.

## Payday loans

Payday loans are small, short-term loans due on your next payday. A typical fee of $15 per $100 borrowed for two weeks works out to an APR of **almost 400%** <Cite id="cfpb-payday" />. Many borrowers roll them over again and again. Almost any other option is better: a credit union's small-dollar loan, a payment plan with the biller, or help from a credit counselor.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Borrow federal before private for school. Federal loans have more protections.
- PSLF can forgive federal loans after 10 years of qualifying public-service work, which fits a city planning career.
- On a car loan, compare total cost, not the monthly payment.
- BNPL is still debt. Payday loans cost nearly 400% APR.
</KeyTakeaways>

<CommonMistakes>
- **Taking private student loans before using up federal options.**
- **Refinancing federal loans into a private loan** if you might use PSLF or income-driven repayment. You lose those protections permanently.
- **Choosing a 72- or 84-month car loan** to afford a more expensive car.
- **Using several BNPL apps at once** and losing track of what's due.
</CommonMistakes>

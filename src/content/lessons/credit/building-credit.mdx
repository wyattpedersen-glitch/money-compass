<WhyItMatters>
You can't get a good score without some credit history, and it takes time to build. The cheapest time to start is now, while you have few bills and no big loans coming up. By the time you're signing a lease for your master's program, you'll want at least a year or two of on-time payments behind you.
</WhyItMatters>

## Starting from nothing

If you have no credit history, the CFPB describes a few common ways to start <Cite id="cfpb-start-rebuild-credit" />:

- **A <Term id="secured-card">secured credit card</Term>**, where you put down a cash deposit (say $500) and can spend up to that amount.
- **A credit-builder loan** from a bank or credit union, which builds credit and savings at the same time.
- **A store or retail card**, which often comes with a low credit limit.

Two other common routes are a student card and becoming an authorized user on a parent's card. If you go the authorized-user route, ask the card issuer whether it reports authorized users to the credit bureaus, and only do it with someone who pays on time.

## The habits that build a score

These follow directly from what goes into the score <Cite id="myfico-score-factors" />:

1. **Pay on time, every time.** Set up autopay for at least the minimum, so one forgotten bill can't hurt you. Payment history is the biggest factor.
2. **Keep balances low compared with your limits.** A common rule of thumb is to use less than 30% of your limits. Lower is better.
3. **Keep your oldest accounts open.** Length of history counts, so closing your first card can lower your score over time.
4. **Apply for new credit sparingly.** Each application usually adds a <Term id="hard-inquiry">hard inquiry</Term>, and several new accounts in a short time can lower your score.

<Example title="Worked example: one card, two years">
You open a student card with a $1,000 limit. You put your phone bill ($40) and some groceries on it each month, about $250 total, and pay the full statement balance automatically.

- **Utilization** is about 25% when the statement closes, and you pay $0 in interest because you pay in full within the <Term id="grace-period">grace period</Term> <Cite id="cfpb-grace-period" />.
- **After a couple of years**, you have a history of on-time payments and a two-year-old account. That's a solid start for an apartment application. You may also be able to ask for a higher limit, which lowers your utilization without spending more.
</Example>

## Myths

- **"You have to carry a balance to build credit."** False. Paying in full every month builds the same history and costs nothing in interest <Cite id="cfpb-credit-myths" />.
- **"Checking your score lowers it."** False. Requesting your own credit report doesn't affect your score <Cite id="cfpb-credit-myths" />.
- **"Closing cards helps your score."** Usually the opposite: it can raise your utilization and eventually shorten your history <Cite id="cfpb-credit-myths" />.
- **"Your income or bank balance affects your score."** They aren't part of it <Cite id="myfico-score-factors" />.

## Repairing credit

If your score has taken a hit, the same habits are the repair: get current on every account, keep paying on time, and bring balances down. Recent behavior counts more than old mistakes, and most negative items drop off after about seven years <Cite id="cfpb-negative-info" />. You can dispute genuine errors yourself, for free <Cite id="ftc-disputing-errors" />.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Start with a student card, a secured card, or by becoming an authorized user.
- Autopay at least the minimum so you never miss a payment.
- Keep card balances well under your limits, and pay in full when you can.
- Keep your first card open, and don't apply for lots of credit at once.
</KeyTakeaways>

<CommonMistakes>
- **Carrying a balance "to build credit"**, which only costs interest.
- **Closing your oldest card** right after getting a better one.
- **Being added as an authorized user** on the card of someone who pays late.
- **Opening several store cards** for one-time discounts.
</CommonMistakes>
